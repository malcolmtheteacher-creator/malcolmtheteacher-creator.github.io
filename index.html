How a lesson works

Read a little. Speak a lot.

The same four steps every time, whatever the subject — built on how memory actually works, so that you do almost all the talking.

1

Start with what you know

Every lesson opens by asking you to recall what you already know, before anything new arrives. Remembering strengthens memory more than looking again.

Practice testing
2

The words you need

The key vocabulary comes first, in context, with a picture or a diagram where one helps, so everything after it makes sense.

Context · dual coding
3

See it in action

A story, a business case, a role play or a grammar point, in small steps: I show it, we try it together, then you try it alone.

Rosenshine's Principles
4

Then you speak

The heart of it: timed speaking tasks where you do most of the talking, explaining why in your own words, with feedback on the task and not on you.

Elaboration · feedback

What I leave out: highlighting and re-reading feel productive but help very little, and matching lessons to a supposed “learning style” has not been shown to improve results.

Based on Dunlosky et al. (2013), Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction and Hattie's Visible Learning.
